# Hybrid Search Techniques: Unifying Dense Embeddings and Sparse BM25
Authors: Elena Rostova, Marcus Thorne, David Chen
Published: Journal of Information Retrieval Systems, 2024

## Page 1: Lexical vs Semantic Retrieval Trade-offs
Dense semantic retrieval models (such as Sentence-BERT, Contriever, and all-MiniLM-L6-v2) represent queries and passages as continuous vectors in low-dimensional space. While dense retrievers excel at understanding semantic synonyms, paraphrases, and conceptual abstractions, they frequently fail on exact numerical values, rare technical acronyms, chemical formulas, and entity identifiers.

Conversely, BM25 (Best Matching 25) relies on probabilistic term frequency and inverse document frequency (TF-IDF). BM25 guarantees robust keyword precision and zero-shot robustness across domain shifts, but struggles with vocabulary mismatch. Hybrid search unifies both paradigms by combining dense embeddings with BM25.

Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) is an empirical rank aggregation method that does not require score normalization across different retrieval models. The formula RRF score = sum(1 / (k + rank)) robustly merges disparate ranking distributions. Setting k = 60 provides optimal balance between high-ranked outliers and low-ranked consensus items.

## Page 2: Empirical Evaluation Across Diverse Datasets
Extensive benchmarking across the BEIR benchmark reveals that hybrid retrieval consistently outscores dense-only models by +4.8% NDCG@10 on specialized scientific corpora. Furthermore, convex score combination using alpha = 0.5 allows system engineers to balance lexical precision against semantic recall.
